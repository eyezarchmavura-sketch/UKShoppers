# UK Shoppers Africa — Verified Retailer Offers Discovery Strategy

**Purpose.** This strategy describes how UK Shoppers Africa can help East African customers discover legitimate UK-store discounts quickly, while avoiding outdated vouchers, fabricated savings, and misleading price claims. It is a product and operations proposal, not a guarantee that the platform will be first to every promotion.

## The strategic position

UK Shoppers Africa should not compete by listing the most deals. It should compete by being the **most trusted East Africa-focused UK offer desk**: every displayed offer should point to a checked retailer or approved partner source, show when it was last verified, state meaningful terms, and explain that UK retail pricing and availability can change at checkout.

> **Customer promise:** “Verified UK offers, checked by our operations team, with the terms and retailer link you need before you request your purchase.”

The platform must never present a discount percentage, “was” price, voucher, or expiry as fact unless it is supported by the retailer or an approved partner source. UK advertising guidance requires savings claims to be non-misleading and rooted in a genuine reference price; it also cautions that “up to” and “from” claims must not exaggerate the likely benefit.[1]

## Where timely offers should come from

| Source class | What it provides | Publish rule | Customer value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Official retailer promotion pages and newsletters** | Retailer sale pages, codes, member events, seasonal campaign terms, and official landing URLs | A team member captures the original URL, checks the UK eligibility and date/terms, then publishes it | The fastest low-cost starting point for named stores already in the directory |
| **Approved retailer or affiliate-partner feeds** | Product name, price, discount-related fields, image, and tracked/deep product link where a retailer permits access | Show only partners for which UK Shoppers Africa is accepted; refresh only on the source’s advertised update pattern; staff approve high-visibility deals | Broad, structured product discovery without manually typing every deal |
| **Retailer social accounts and customer tips** | Early signals that a promotion may exist | Treat as a lead only; do not publish until an official retailer page or approved partner feed confirms it | Helps the team discover campaign launches quickly without turning rumours into offers |
| **Paid retailer collaborations** | Campaign brief, approved images and terms, exclusive code or landing page | Label clearly as an advertisement or paid partnership and attach the exact approved terms | Allows a distinct “partner offer” placement without confusing it with independent editorial picks |

An affiliate product feed can be a legitimate second-stage data source. Awin’s publisher documentation states that feeds can include a deep product link, product name and description, price including discounts and shipping, images, and product-specific attributes; it also provides a last-update time so a publisher can avoid needless repeated downloads.[2] Availability and permissions remain retailer- and partner-specific; the platform must not assume that every listed retailer provides a usable feed.

## The non-negotiable verification workflow

Every offer should move through five controlled states: **lead**, **checked**, **scheduled**, **published**, and **expired**. The existing staff workspace can represent the latter stages with draft, published, and expired records; the small additions below make the workflow audit-ready.

| Step | Owner | Required evidence | What appears to customers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Capture | Offer researcher | Original retailer/partner link, source type, time found, and a note of the stated terms | Nothing yet |
| 2. Verify | Operations reviewer | Opened UK source, offer description, applicable code if any, start/end date or “while stocks last,” and country/eligibility note | Nothing yet |
| 3. Publish | Staff member | Verified timestamp, retailer link, short plain-language summary, terms link, and expiry rule | Offer card and detail page |
| 4. Re-check | Operations reviewer | Retailer source still active; code and displayed details still match | “Checked [time]” refreshed or offer expired |
| 5. Expire | Staff member or approved source update | Expiry reached, source removed, stock/terms changed, or verification fails | Removed from public results; retained internally for audit |

The public card should always show **“Verified [date/time]”**, **“Ends [date]”** or **“While retailer stock lasts”**, **“Open retailer terms”**, and the official retailer destination. It should not manufacture a percentage saving from a crossed-out price. When a customer follows a tracked or partner link, the card and deal page need a clear, adjacent label such as **“Advertisement / partner link — UK Shoppers Africa may earn a commission.”** The ASA states that marketing must be obviously identifiable and that affiliate-linked content must make its commercial intent clear; vague or bottom-of-page disclosures are not enough.[3]

## The customer experience

The improved hero panel should show only the three most recently verified, currently live opportunities. Each card should use a store icon, short offer title, category, verification time, end condition, and a single action: **View offer and retailer terms**. Avoid an unqualified “best deal” label because it suggests a comparison that may not have been made.

The full **Seasonal offers** page should provide filters for retailer, category, destination relevance, newest verified, ending soon, and UK sale period. A deal detail page should show the official terms, a UK price if explicitly provided by the source, the service-fee/shipping reminder, and a **Request this item** action that carries the retailer link into the staff-reviewed purchase request. This protects the no-invented-quote principle: customers can see a retailer offer, but UK Shoppers Africa still confirms purchasing and delivery costs before taking payment.

For the four supported public languages, keep the offer facts source-faithful. Translate the explanatory labels and terms summary, but retain the original code, retailer title, price currency, end date, and source link. Show a compact message such as: “Retailer price and availability are confirmed by the store at checkout.”

## Three workable launch approaches

| Approach | Trade-offs | Cost | Setup complexity |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Curated Offer Desk — recommended first** | Staff check official retailer pages/newsletters and publish high-confidence offers. It is not automatic, but it is fast, controlled, and works before partnership approvals. | Low; uses the current offers workspace and staff time | Low |
| **Approved Partner Feed** | Brings larger product and price coverage after publisher approval. Feed fields and refresh rules vary by retailer, and affiliate disclosure becomes mandatory where commission applies. | Usually commercial/commission-led; partner terms vary | Medium |
| **Automated Offer Watch** | Can flag source changes faster, but only for sources that permit programmatic access or provide approved feeds/APIs. It needs ongoing source monitoring, error handling, and human verification before publication. | Medium to high, depending on source access and monitoring frequency | High |

The recommended path is to start with the curated desk for the top 10–15 stores, using seasonal launch calendars and official UK retailer sources. In parallel, apply to appropriate approved partner programmes. Once a feed or API is contractually available, connect it as a **lead generator**—not an automatic publisher—until its records have passed the same terms and expiry checks.

## A practical 30-day rollout

| Period | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Set the top-store priority list, assign a reviewer, define deal-card fields, set a “last verified” service level, and publish the first manually checked offers. |
| **Week 2** | Add offer-detail pages, source evidence in the staff workspace, retailer/category filters, and an internal expired-offer review queue. |
| **Weeks 3–4** | Apply to suitable approved retailer/affiliate partnerships; test one permitted feed or approved data source; measure clicks, requests, and conversion to approved quotes. |
| **After validation** | Offer opt-in alerts by email or WhatsApp only after customers choose the channel and the related provider configuration, consent records, and message templates are in place. |

## Decisions for the co-owner discussion

The first decision is whether UK Shoppers Africa should begin with a focused **Top 10 verified store desk** or wait for broader partner-feed coverage. The focused desk is the safer recommendation because it delivers quality immediately and produces evidence of which stores and categories customers actually request.

The second decision is the customer promise: use **“verified by our team”** and **“checked at [time]”** rather than “before anyone else.” The latter cannot be guaranteed and creates unnecessary customer-expectation risk. Finally, decide whether affiliate revenue is part of the commercial model; if yes, include clear per-offer disclosure from day one and keep the buying advice honest.
